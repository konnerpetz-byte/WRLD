# WRLD — competitive review and premium plan

_October 2026. Prices are what each company lists publicly and change often._

## The short answer

**Is WRLD a good idea?** It's a good product idea in a hard business.

- **Already done many times:** "trip planner with an itinerary and a map" (Wanderlog, TripIt, Mindtrip, Stippl, Layla, Rhyme and dozens more). On its own, that would be redone over and over.
- **Not done together:** points/award search (PointsYeah, Seats.aero, Roame, point.me) and group trip planning live in separate apps today.
- **Why the category is hard:**
  - People take only a few trips a year, so apps are opened rarely, forgotten, and hard to charge for monthly.
  - Most planning startups fail on retention and monetization, not on the product.
- **Where WRLD stands out:**
  - Days built around real flight times.
  - Official ticket pages and time slots filled in automatically.
  - Reels and TikToks turned into itinerary items.
  - One budget that counts both cash and points.
  - Built for a group, with chat and cost splitting.
- **The wedge:** _the trip planner for points travelers and their friends._
  - Points enthusiasts travel more often and already pay for tools (PointsYeah $99.99/yr, Seats.aero $99.99/yr).
  - They talk in reachable communities.
  - Their friends become free users through invites.

## What to make premium

**Rule of thumb:** never charge for what spreads the app. Invites, group chat, shared plans, voting and cost splitting stay free. Every friend invited is a new user. Charge for what saves serious travelers money or time, and for what costs WRLD money to run.

### Free

- Unlimited trips, cities, itinerary, map
- Group invites, chat, voting, checklists and cost splitting
- Saved spots from social videos
- Official ticket matching and auto time slots
- Budget with manual points balances

### WRLD Pro: suggested $5.99/mo or $49/yr (in line with TripIt Pro, under PointsYeah)

1. **Award search and alerts.** Points flights and hotels, with "your points can book this" using your wallet. This is the most valuable piece for the target users, and competitors charge $99.99/yr for it alone.
2. **Connected points wallet** that syncs automatically (AwardWallet costs money per user).
3. **Flight status and disruption alerts** (needs a paid flight-data feed).
4. **Import bookings from email** (forward or Gmail sync).
5. **Offline trips and maps** in the iOS and Android apps.
6. **Route optimization** for each day.
7. **AI planner** with unlimited use (each request costs money).
8. **Exports:** PDF trip book, calendar, Google Maps.

### Trip Pass: suggested $9.99 per trip, one time

The organizer unlocks Pro features for **everyone on one trip**. This fits how rarely most people travel, and the organizer's friends try Pro without subscribing.

### Revenue beyond subscriptions

- **Affiliate commissions** on activities (Viator, GetYourGuide) and stays (Booking, Expedia programs). These usually earn more than subscriptions in travel apps.
- **Later:** credit-card referral offers fit the points audience but come with legal and disclosure rules. Get advice first.

## Risks to plan for

- **Award data:** PointsYeah and Seats.aero run their own data collection. WRLD needs a licensed source or a partner before promising award search.
- **AwardWallet API access** has to be approved.
- **Official attraction list** has to be kept current (prices change yearly). Budget time for it, or pay for a data provider.
- **Cold start:** test with 20–30 points travelers planning a real group trip before paying for marketing. Watch whether their friends join from invites.
